# Map styles: one engine, a catalogue, a picker

GoWay draws its basemap in several STYLES — Standard, Streets, the muted
Light and Dark data basemaps, and (in the next changes) Outdoors, Navigation
and Monochrome — each in one or more LIGHTS: day, dawn, dusk, night. Every
document is generated by ONE style engine from ONE layer list; no style is a
hand-written JSON file, and no layer list is copied.

| Piece | Where |
| --- | --- |
| The catalogue: style ids, the lights each is published in, paths, the light modes | `packages/frontend/lib/map/style/catalogue.ts` |
| A style's switches over the shared layers | `lib/map/style/options.ts` |
| A (style, light)'s colours: 2D palette, 3D ground, clay, street detail | `lib/map/style/themes.ts` |
| A style's NIGHT under the city sky and on the globe: the night's light, the sky dome, street light, floodlights | `lib/map/style/nightSky.ts` (with `chroma.ts`, the recipes' shared colour functions) |
| The one layer list | `lib/map/style/layers.ts` |
| The documents, the catalogue index, the checks | `scripts/build-map-style.ts` → `public/map/styles/` |
| The picker's thumbnails (real renderer) | `scripts/build-map-thumbs.ts` → `public/map/styles/thumbs/` |
| The picker | `features/explore/MapStyleButton.tsx`, `MapStylePicker.tsx` |
| Which document a map loads | `components/map/useMapStyle.ts` |
| The contact sheet (every style, place and zoom) | `scripts/shoot-map-styles.ts` |
| Tests | `lib/map/__tests__/mapStyles.test.ts` (+ the store, embed and 3D-ground tests) |

## The catalogue

| Style | Lights | What it is for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `standard` (default) | day, dawn, dusk, night | The everyday map: GoWay's measured, Apple-grade look (`palette.ts`), POIs in their category colours, the detailed 3D city. House numbers from z18. |
| `streets` | day, night | The road map: a coloured road hierarchy (amber expressways, honey trunks, cream primaries; ember at night), denser POIs (half again as many marks and names, the minor tier from z16), footprints from z14, street names as written, house numbers from z17. |
| `outdoors` | day, night | The terrain map: its own hillshade and contour lines from GoWay's elevation tiles (index lines and heights from z12), bare rock and glaciers, protected areas' dashed edges, trails coloured by difficulty from z12, lifts and cable cars, peaks with their heights from z9, and the shelters, campsites, drinking water and crags the other styles leave out. |
| `navigation` | day, night | For driving: roads 1.25× wider with dark casings by day, amber expressways and honey trunks, a cool quiet ground that leaves the route line the most saturated thing on screen, one-way arrows, names as written a point larger, and only the stops a driver makes (fuel, charging, parking, rental — and stations). GoWay has no live traffic data; the style is ready for a traffic overlay (cool roads, no red in the network) but draws none. |
| `monochrome` | day, night | Standard with every colour at 6% of its chroma — lightness, so the whole hierarchy, kept — built FROM Standard so it follows every change to it; the 3D clay and street detail go grey with it. |
| `light` | day (fixed) | A quiet light basemap for somebody else's data: Bloom's neutral greys, no POIs, arterial names only, no one-way arrows. |
| `dark` | night (fixed) | Its dark twin. |

Every document is served at a stable URL —
`https://goway.to/map/styles/<style>/<light>.json` — and the catalogue itself
at `https://goway.to/map/styles/index.json` (every style, its lights, each
URL). `/map/goway-light.json` and `/map/goway-dark.json` are still published,
byte-identical copies of Standard by day and by night, for the native builds
and integrations that name them.

## Audit: how the basemap was made before this

- **Tiles**: GoWay's own PMTiles planet in the OpenMapTiles v3 schema
  (Planetiler 0.10.2, default profile, z0–14), served by the Worker from R2.
  16 source-layers; `housenumber` and `mountain_peak` were in the tiles and
  drawn by nothing.
- **Style**: `buildGowayMapStyle(appearance)` — one layer list
  (`layers.ts`, ~70 layers) and two measured palettes (`palette.ts`, sampled
  off Apple Maps captures and walked to a 4.5:1 type floor), rendered to
  `public/map/goway-{light,dark}.json`. Light/dark followed the app's Bloom
  theme and nothing else.
- **Bloom tokens**: deliberately NOT in the cartography (`palette.ts` argues
  why: interface colours and map colours are different jobs).
- **Night**: the 2D documents never changed with the sun. The real sun darkens
  the map through MULTIPLY passes drawn over it — the globe's night side below
  z13 (`globeNightLayer.web.ts`) and the city sky's ground pass from z12–13
  (`engine/city3d/tsl/skyPasses.ts`) — and relights `building-3d` and the 3D
  city. `nightFactor` is 1 on the dark map.
- **3D city**: its building ramp and trees derive from the cartographic
  palette; the detailed look's clay (`CITY_MATERIALS`), street detail
  (`STREET_PALETTES`) and ground (`CITY_GROUND_PALETTES`, applied as paint
  overrides from the document's `metadata`) were per appearance.
- **Relief**: Mapterhorn's DEM (Terrarium WebP, z0–12) through the Worker:
  hillshade (web and native), 3D terrain, sky and globe (web), added at
  runtime, never in the documents.

What each style needs that the engine now supplies: per-style options over the
shared layers (density, house numbers, label case, footprint zoom), per-style
palettes for the 2D map AND the 3D city, a light per document, documents at
stable URLs, a picker that names them, and a device choice that persists.
What the schema does not carry (contours, trail difficulty, pistes) is under
[Data gaps](#data-gaps).

## The engine

```
catalogue.ts   MAP_STYLES: id → lights it is published in, follows/fixed
     │
themes.ts      styleTheme({style, light}) → { palette, options, cityGround,
     │                                         materials, streets }
layers.ts      buildLayers(palette, source, options) — THE layer list
     │
index.ts       buildGowayMapStyle(selection) → a MapLibre document
     │
build-map-style.ts → public/map/styles/<style>/<light>.json, index.json,
                     the legacy pair; validated and drift-checked
```

**One palette source.** A theme is everything a style changes. The 2D map
reads `palette`; the detailed 3D city's ground reads `cityGround` (diffed
against `palette` into the document's `goway:city3d-ground` overrides); the
3D city's building ramp and trees are derived from `palette`; its clay is
`materials` and its street detail `streets`. `city3dLayer.web.ts` takes the
selection (`AttachCity3dOptions.style`) and reads all of them from
`styleTheme`, so the buildings, trees and pavements always match the style
they stand on.

**Where each style's colours come from.**

- Standard day and night are `palette.ts`'s measured palettes, untouched —
  the engine reproduces the old documents exactly, plus house numbers.
- Dawn and dusk are the day map lit by the low sun: lightness × 0.975 / 0.94
  and a push along OKLab's chroma plane toward rose (dawn) or amber (dusk),
  water deepened toward the night's. A mix toward the night palette was tried
  first and crossed the grey middle of the cream–violet axis (mud on
  Barcelona); the contact sheet decided.
- Streets keeps Standard's ground, water and type and recolours the road
  network (`STREETS_ROADS`); at night its city streets are a step deeper than
  Standard's so their white names clear 4.5:1 on the fill itself.
- Light and Dark are built from Bloom's neutral `mono` tokens, READ from
  `@oxy.so/bloom/preset-vars` (never restated): land is Bloom's background
  (light) or surface (dark), streets its card/muted, type its
  muted-foreground; water and green are a whisper of hue mixed in. A Bloom
  release that moves its greys moves these maps.
- Every derived palette's type is walked back to 4.5:1 on what it is read on
  (`enforceTypeContrast`, the method `palette.ts` used by hand); the tests
  hold every derived (style, light) to it.

**One map in every style.** Every document emits an ordered subset of
`GOWAY_STYLE_LAYER_IDS` with the label anchor always present, so an overlay
anchored by id lands in the same stratum in every style; a style's lights emit
identical ids; no two documents draw the same thing. `map:style:check`
enforces all three on top of its existing checks (the MapLibre spec, the
schema, fonts, first-party origins, throwing filters, drift).

## The light

Settings → Map → Light (and the picker): **App** (the default — the app's
theme, as the map always did), **Sun** (the real sun over the settled camera:
day above 4°, night below −6°, dawn or dusk between, morning and evening told
apart by azimuth; re-read on every settle and every five minutes, changing the
document only when the light changes), **Day**, **Night** (an always-light or
always-dark map, whatever the app's theme). A style without dawn and dusk draws
twilight as day while the sun is up and night once it has set. A fixed-light
style (Light, Dark) ignores the mode — and is not darkened by the globe's
night pass either: its night side would be a different map.

The light is the real sun, not the time-of-day scrubber: changing the document
rebuilds the web engine (a few hundred ms, the camera kept), which is right a
handful of times a day and wrong forty times a second under a slider. The
scrubber keeps relighting the scene through the sky's passes, as before.

**The night is the style's.** The city sky's night (its one light, the
sky dome, the street light on the walls, the floodlights) and the globe's
night side come from `nightSky.ts`: Standard's art-directed blue-violet, and
every other style's derived from it by the colour function its recipe applies
to its palette. Monochrome's is 6% chroma, the data basemaps' and
Navigation's quiet. Each light keeps its brightness, so a style changes the
night's colour, never how dark it is. Monochrome at night is achromatic,
windows included (`lib/map/__tests__/nightSky.test.ts`).

**What darkens the map with the sun draws only what the document does not.**
A dawn or dusk document already draws the low sun's light. It stands for the
middle of its band (`TWILIGHT_DOCUMENT_DEG`, −1°). Over it, the globe's
night and the city sky's ground light darken the map only as the sun goes on
below that, relative to the document's own light
(`catalogue.ts` → `presetDocumentLight`). Before, both darkenings were drawn,
the document's and the pass's, and a dusk map went a dull blue-grey. Measured
at Madrid with the sun 3.2° down, Standard dusk at z9: the dusk colours now
stand, darkened only past −1°.

A style change rebuilds the MapLibre engine at the camera the old one had —
as a relief toggle always did; a theme change now does too (it used to jump
back to `initialViewport`). Native swaps `mapStyle` on the same view.

## The picker

A layers button (Bloom `Fab`, `RiStackLine`) under the settings gear in the
top-right stack opens a Bloom `Dialog` — a bottom sheet on a phone, a card on
a wide window — with:

1. **Style**: a tile per style, two a row on a phone and four on a wide
   window, each a THUMBNAIL of that style's own document rendered by MapLibre
   through the embed (`scripts/build-map-thumbs.ts`: Barcelona's Ciutadella
   and port at z13.4, 480 × 320 shown at 240 × 160, the mark and the folded
   credit included), in day or night as the map is now. Each is pinned in
   `thumbs/manifest.json` to a hash of its document's layers, and CI's
   `map:thumbs:check` fails when a style changes without new photographs.
2. **Light**: the four modes above (disabled, with a note, on a fixed style).
3. **Map details**: the registry's own switches for the 3D city, terrain,
   simulated traffic and (where offered) live data — read and written through
   the map-feature store exactly as Settings writes them.

Choices apply at once on the map behind and are kept on this device in the
map-feature document (`goway.mapFeatures`: `style`, `light`; the defaults are
not stored). Settings → Map shows the style with "Choose…" (which opens the
picker) and the Light control. The picker ships in the settings modal's lazy
chunk (`MapSettingsModal.tsx` re-exports it): the feature labels both use stay
out of the first-paint bundle.

Every style keeps the mark bottom-left and the compact credit bottom-right:
`MapCanvas` renders both for every document, and the thumbnails show them too.

## For integrators

- **A style by id**: `https://goway.to/map/styles/<style>/<light>.json` is a
  complete MapLibre document (tiles, glyphs, sprite on goway.to, the
  OSM/OpenMapTiles credit on its source). The ids and lights are the
  catalogue's; `https://goway.to/map/styles/index.json` lists them.
- **The embed**: `/frame?…&style=streets&light=night`. Only GoWay's own ids
  are accepted (an unknown one draws Standard, a URL is never followed); a
  light the style lacks resolves to the nearest it has; `theme=` remains the
  short spelling of day and night. The embed never follows the visitor's own
  map setting: the embedder decides.
- **`@goway.to/sdk`**: the vocabulary and the catalogue are defined ONCE, in
  `packages/contracts/src/map-style.ts` (`MAP_STYLE_IDS`, `MAP_LIGHT_PRESETS`,
  `MAP_STYLE_CATALOGUE`, `resolveMapStyle`, `mapStylePath`, and the zod schema
  of `index.json`); the app's catalogue reads them from there. The SDK
  exports them, plus `client.links.mapStyle(style, light?)` (the document URL
  for `new maplibregl.Map({ style })` or MapLibre Native's `mapStyle`),
  `client.links.embed(viewport, { style, light })` and
  `client.links.mapStyleIndex()` with `parseMapStyleIndex` — open, so a style
  GoWay adds later is listed rather than refused. The documents are static
  files on GoWay's origin, not `/api/v1` operations, so there is no
  `API_OPERATIONS` entry and nothing for `bun run openapi` to add:

  ```ts
  import { createGoWayClient } from '@goway.to/sdk';
  const goway = createGoWayClient();
  new maplibregl.Map({ container, style: goway.links.mapStyle('streets', 'night') });
  ```

## Imagery: Satellite and Hybrid

Imagery is a style, not a mode beside the catalogue (`docs/MAP_SATELLITE.md`):

1. `satellite` and `hybrid` are `MAP_STYLES` entries (`imagery`, one fixed
   `day` light, `offer: 'development'` until the imagery is published), with
   labels in `mapStyleLabels.ts` and recipes in `themes.ts` (Standard's options
   plus `imagery`, over the night palette's light type);
2. `buildGowayMapStyle` hands a recipe with `options.imagery` to
   `applyImageryStyle` (`lib/map/style/imagery.ts`): the three imagery rasters
   (`GOWAY_STYLE_LAYER_IDS.imagery`, right above `background`) under the
   document, the ground fills it replaces dropped, and for hybrid the roads
   (thinned), boundaries and labels kept with dark halos for photographs;
3. the imagery origin comes in `MapStyleEndpoints.imageryOrigin`, and the
   credit through `MapAttribution` (`useImageryCredits`);
4. the picker and the embed ask `isMapStyleOffered`, so production lists them
   once the `offer` line goes.

The store, the URLs and the thumbnails pick them up from the catalogue
(`map:thumbs --only=satellite,hybrid` takes just theirs).

## Data gaps

What a style wants that the served OpenMapTiles schema does not have:

| Want | In the tiles? | Plan |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Contours (Outdoors) | No — relief is a raster DEM | Done: traced at the edge from GoWay's elevation tiles ([Contours](#contours)) |
| Trail difficulty (`sac_scale`) | No — `transportation` carries `subclass`, `mtb_scale` (0–6) and `surface`, not `sac_scale` | Outdoors colours trails by `mtb_scale` (0–1, 2–3, 4+), and town footways stay quiet; `sac_scale` needs a Planetiler profile change in `scripts/build-map-tiles.ts` (one attribute on `transportation` for `path`) and a tile rebuild — scoped follow-up |
| Ski pistes | No (`landuse`/`landcover` have no piste class) | Same follow-up: `piste:type` lines into a new source-layer |
| Peaks with elevations | Yes — `mountain_peak` (`name`, `ele`, `rank`, `class`) | Outdoors |
| Huts, viewpoints, springs | Huts and viewpoints as `poi` classes; springs not in the default profile | Outdoors draws the ones present |
| Protected areas | Yes — the `park` layer | Drawn in every style; Outdoors outlines them |
| POI icons | The SDF sprite has 16 category icons; no layer uses `icon-image` | Follow-up for Streets and Standard: a disc-and-glyph icon needs either a composited non-SDF sprite per category or two collision-linked layers |

## Contours

The OpenMapTiles schema has no contours; Outdoors traces them from the same
Mapterhorn DEM (Terrarium WebP, z0–12) the hillshade and 3D terrain read, and
the choice of WHERE was made for phones:

| Where | Phones | Native, integrators | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| In the browser (maplibre-contour's protocol in a web worker) | download nine DEM tiles per contour tile and trace them on the device's CPU and battery | none: MapLibre Native has no protocol hook, and a style URL cannot carry one | nothing server-side |
| A planet batch (contour tiles z9–14 into R2) | a vector tile | yes | a multi-day job, hundreds of GB, rebuilt with every DEM |
| **At the edge, on demand** (`worker/contours.js`) | **a vector tile** | **yes — one URL** | the first request for a tile traces it; every later one is an edge-cache hit; storage only for what is seen |

So `https://goway.to/map/contours/{z}/{x}/{y}.pbf` (z9–14, over-zoomed above)
is made by the Worker: it fetches the nine DEM tiles around a tile through the
terrain route (the R2 archive when configured, Mapterhorn upstream
otherwise), decodes them with libwebp compiled to WebAssembly
(`worker/webpDecode.js`, `@jsquash/webp`, 138 KB — a Worker has no image
decoder), traces them with maplibre-contour's `LocalDemManager` (the same
marching squares, seam stitching and MVT encoder its browser protocol runs)
and caches the tile for a year, keyed by the DEM archive. One source-layer,
`contours`: `ele` in metres, `level` 1 on index lines; intervals 200/1000 m at
z9–10, 100/500 at z11, 50/250 at z12, 20/100 at z13, 10/50 at z14.

Measured: the Worker bundles to 313 KiB (90 KiB gz) with the decoder; under
`wrangler dev --local` (workerd) the Chamonix z12 tile took 0.73 s cold,
including nine upstream DEM fetches, and its neighbour 0.22 s; 68 KB, the
densest case (the Mont Blanc massif at 50 m). The bytes are identical to the
Node path `scripts/serve-map-build.ts` serves locally with the same module.
`worker/__tests__/contours.test.js` traces a synthetic mountain through the
real tracer.

The style document carries the relief itself — a `raster-dem` source over
`/map/terrain`, a `hillshade` layer, the contour source and its three layers —
so every renderer that loads it draws them: native and integrators included.
The relief toggle's own hillshade steps aside on a style that has one
(`reliefLayer.web.ts`, `MapCanvas.native.tsx`), and the elevation credit shows
whenever such a style draws, whatever the Terrain switch says
(`MapAttribution`'s `elevation`, from the catalogue's `relief`).

## The quality bar

The contact sheet is made with `scripts/shoot-map-styles.ts` against a local
production export (`serve-map-build.ts --upstream=https://goway.to`), at
Barcelona, Manhattan, Tokyo, Chamonix (the Alps) and São Paulo, z5/12/16/18,
every style in every light, the clock fixed (local solar noon for day, 23:00
for night), SwiftShader. The sheet for each change is linked from its PR.

**Performance.** A style change is colour and density. Colour costs
nothing; density does, and the only style that draws more than Standard is
Streets (half again as many POI marks and names, the minor tier and house
numbers a zoom sooner, footprints from z14): measured on the phone profile,
~14% more draw calls at the Eixample. So density is a QualityProfile knob
(`basemap.density`, AGENTS.md: a new cost is a profile knob): on the LOW tier
a dense style is drawn at Standard's density. A document is a static file, so
the build writes the difference into its own metadata (`goway:quality-low`:
the same layers' filters, zoom ranges and sizes at Standard's density, from
running the one layer list twice — `lib/map/style/qualityOverrides.ts`), and
the web map applies it while the profile asks and undoes it exactly when it
stops (`components/map/qualityOverrides.ts`, tested). Medium and high draw
the style as designed. Every other style is at most Standard's density
(Light and Dark draw fewer layers than Standard: no POIs, no house numbers,
no one-way arrows). The CI frame-budget gate (`frameBudget.test.ts`) measures
the 3D city, which no style changes the cost of. The bench numbers for each
change are in its PR (`bench:map --device=pixel6a --quality=low
--scenes=eixample,manhattan --style=<style>`).

## Limits

- The city sky's night (its moonlit blue ambient and the ground pass) is
  Standard's art direction in every style; Streets' night is grounded on
  Standard's slate for that reason, and the Dark basemap with the detailed 3D
  city at night still takes a blue cast from the sky. A per-style night tint
  belongs in `engine/city3d/atmosphere.ts` and is a follow-up for the sky's
  owners.
- A development server (`expo start`) has no `/map/contours` until the route
  is deployed: Outdoors draws without contours there. A local production
  export served with `serve-map-build.ts --upstream=…` makes them.
- Native was typechecked, not run: the style swap is a `mapStyle` prop change
  on the same view, which MapLibre Native supports; the thumbnails load by
  absolute URL.
